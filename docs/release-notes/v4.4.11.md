# jawata-mcp v4.4.11

**The first recall after a restart finds its work already done, a write stops re-reading the whole store, a recall that got no answer is counted, and the screen a rule is accepted from shows its condition.**

## What went wrong

- **The first recall after a restart was thrown away.** Measured on a restart over a store of 10,189 rows (private engine, vector module on, the same store directory reopened): the first recall took 709 ms and gave no answer inside the hook's 200 ms budget; recalls 2 to 14 answered in 120 to 234 ms. The first call built three things while the hook waited: the corpus snapshot (every row read once), the stored vectors, and the embedder.
- **Every write made its own steering lookup read and re-index the whole store.** A write moves the store's change stamp, so the next lookup rebuilt the corpus snapshot from every row. Sampled over 10,189 rows that was 43% of a write's own lookup. Under load, 5 of 20 writes beside a heavy concurrent process said "the store was NOT consulted"; 0 of 40 reads did.
- **A recall that got no answer from the store left no trace** apart from the sentence in that one response, so how often it happened could not be read from anywhere.
- **The list a rule is accepted from hid when the rule applies and who it binds.** `experience(kind=list)` rows carried the rule's sentence, lane, version and date, and neither its `situation` nor its `operation`, although the engine refuses a proposal without both. Seven proposed rules were judged without a single condition on screen.

## What changes

- **A warm-up at resident start** (`RecallWarmUp`, on a daemon thread right after the store opens) reads the corpus, loads the vectors and runs one embedding. On the same 10,189-row store the first recall after a restart answers: 256 ms measured client-side, recalls 2 to 14 at 118 to 186 ms. The warm-up itself takes 742 ms there.
- **After a write the corpus snapshot is assembled from the rows that changed.** A lane whose rows did not change keeps its word index; only the rows whose change mark moved are fetched; any doubt (no marks, a short fetch, more than 200 changed rows, `-Djawata.corpus.max.incremental`) falls back to the full read, and a test holds the result equal to a full read after every kind of write. Lesson writes on the same store: median 418 to 306 ms, 90th percentile 453 to 341 ms.
- **Unanswered recalls are counted per surface** and shown in the quality statistics as `recalls_not_consulted`, apart from a surface that stayed silent.
- **A list row carries `situation` when the entry has one and, for a rule, `operation`** (`all` where the store holds none, so an empty binding cannot read as "binds nobody").

## Known limits

- **A call that arrives while the warm-up runs still waits.** Fired the instant the port opened, the first call took 551 ms and still had no answer inside 200 ms. The window is the resident's first second after it opens its port; it is shortened, not closed.
- **All timings are from a synthetic store of 10,189 rows on one machine,** not from a real store and not under load. The share of recalls inside 200 ms on a released resident is read from the new counter after this release.
- **The engine still checks only that a rule's situation is not blank,** not that it can be checked against a message or a call.

Works with jawata-studio 4.4.6.
