# jawata-mcp v3.11.1

The knowledge layer can no longer hang your session, and it can no longer tell
you "nothing is known" when the truth is "I could not look".

## "Could not answer" is now its own answer (#37)

The experience store had two ways of saying nothing, and only one of them was a
fact. A store that genuinely holds nothing for a cue answers an absence — that
is an observation. But a store whose read had wedged produced a bare timeout,
and a store serving its emergency in-memory fallback answered "No known
knowledge for this cue" — the same sentence, for a corpus that was never the
real one. Every consumer read all three as the same thing.

Recall and the session primer now answer `KNOWLEDGE_UNAVAILABLE`, as a typed
error in both formats, when the store throws, when it exceeds its deadline, or
when an empty answer comes from the degraded fallback. The message says what it
is: not an absence, nothing established either way, proceed without recall and
say so. A real hit from a degraded store is still a hit — only the emptiness
was a lie, and only the emptiness is gone.

## Your deadline travels with your call

A wedged read used to hold callers for as long as the caller's own transport
allowed, then fail anonymously. A retrieval now runs under a deadline and
releases the caller with the typed answer when it blows. The deadline is the
call's, not the server's: pass `budget_ms` when your own timeout is shorter
than the 15-second default, and the typed answer arrives inside your window.
It clamps one way — you may buy a faster answer, never a longer wait.

## Small values stop crossing the wire as streams

The measured incident was a reader parked in a socket read for 58 minutes,
holding the store lock, with 66 threads queued behind it — streaming a value
that should never have been a stream. Entry text and embeddings now return
inline (`MAX_LENGTH_INPLACE_LOB`), verified against the database's own settings
table, not against the source. Inline values cannot hang mid-read.

## Honest limits, stated

- The deadline bounds recall and the primer — the paths agents sit on. The
  store's other operations (record, list, stats, compact) are not yet bounded;
  that is the next release's structural work, and #37 stays open until it
  lands.
- H2 2.2.224 silently discards `Connection.setNetworkTimeout` — its method body
  is literally `return`. A tripwire test pins that fact, so the day the driver
  starts honouring it, the test fails and tells us the workaround can go.
- Found on the way, filed as #38: a closed in-memory store silently re-opens as
  a fresh empty database on the next read.

## Also

- The debugger's recall-before-probe now reports an unreadable knowledge layer
  in its steering instead of proceeding as if nothing were on record.
- A store serving its degraded fallback is visible through the interface
  (`degradedNotice`), and the recovery wrapper forwards the question to the
  store it wraps instead of answering for it.
